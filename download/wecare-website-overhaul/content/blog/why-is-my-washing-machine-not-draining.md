# Why Is My Washing Machine Not Draining? 7 Common Causes & Fixes

A washing machine that fills, washes and spins but refuses to drain leaves you with a drum full of grey water, soaking clothes and a stalled laundry day. In Jammu households this problem shows up more often than elsewhere — heavy winter kurta-pajamas, woollen layers and thick local shawls shed far more lint and fibre than summer clothing, and the mineral-rich mountain water we get from local supply lines leaves sediment that slowly blocks narrow drain passages. The good news is that most drainage failures trace back to one of seven common causes, and several of them you can safely check yourself before booking a repair. This guide walks through each one in plain language so you know what to inspect, what to leave alone, and when it makes sense to call a certified technician.

## 1. Clogged Drain Pump Filter — The Most Common Culprit

The drain pump filter sits at the front bottom of most front-load washers behind a small flap. Its job is to catch coins, lint, hairpins and fabric fibre before they reach the pump, but in Jammu Region homes it fills up faster than the manual suggests because of dense woollen lint and the dust our kurta-pajamas and carpets carry. A fully blocked filter stops water leaving the drum and triggers an "OE" or "drain error" code on LG and Samsung machines. Cleaning it takes five minutes — switch off the machine, open the flap, unscrew the filter, rinse it under a tap and refit it. Do this every two months in winter, and you will prevent the majority of drainage failures before they start.

## 2. Kinked or Frozen Drain Hose

The drain hose is the flexible pipe that carries used water from the pump to your bathroom drain or sink. If the hose gets pushed against a wall, kinked during a deep clean, or frozen during a harsh Jammu Region winter night in an unheated utility room, water simply cannot pass through. Check that the hose runs straight with no sharp bends, is not crushed behind the machine, and exits at the height recommended in your manual — usually between 60 and 100 cm from the floor. In sub-zero Jammu Region winters, a hose resting against an exterior wall can freeze solid overnight; insulating it with a foam sleeve or moving the machine away from the wall solves this completely.

## 3. Faulty Drain Pump Motor

When the filter and hose are clear but the machine still refuses to drain, the drain pump itself is the next suspect. The pump contains a small electric motor with an impeller that spins to push water out — and after years of use, especially with sediment-heavy Jammu water, the bearings wear out or the impeller seizes. You will usually hear a faint humming noise with no water movement, or in worse cases, complete silence when the pump should be running. This is a part that requires a trained technician to replace, but the part itself is affordable. We carry compatible pumps for LG, Samsung, Whirlpool, IFB and Bosch in our service vans for same-day replacement.

## 4. Coin Trap Blockage

Many top-load and some front-load machines have a separate coin trap upstream of the pump — a small chamber designed to catch coins, keys and buttons before they damage the impeller. Coins from shalwar kameez pockets, hairpins, small stones from winter footwear and even chai coins collect here over months of use. Once the chamber fills, water flow slows to a trickle or stops entirely. Removing the trap, cleaning it out and refitting it is a 10-minute job for most models, but it requires tilting the machine forward carefully. If you are not comfortable lifting a fully-loaded washer, leave this to a technician — the trap itself rarely needs replacement, only cleaning.

## 5. Lid or Door Switch Failure

Top-load washing machines use a lid switch as a safety device — the machine will not drain or spin unless the lid is firmly closed and the switch registers it. Over time, and especially with the moisture-rich environment of a Jammu Region bathroom utility corner, the switch contacts corrode or the plastic actuator breaks. The machine washes normally but refuses to drain at the end of the cycle, sometimes accompanied by a click that does not trigger the cycle to continue. A technician can test the switch with a multimeter in two minutes and replace it on the spot. This is one of the cheapest repairs you will ever book — usually under ₹800 including the part.

## 6. Excessive Detergent Causing Over-Sudsing

Jammu households often use more detergent than the dose recommended on the pack, assuming hard water demands it. The result is over-sudsing — a thick foam layer that the drain pump struggles to push through the narrow outlet hose. The pump runs longer, overheats, and in many cases the machine aborts the drain cycle and flashes an error. Switching to an HE (high-efficiency) detergent and using only the recommended dose — usually one to two tablespoons for a full load — eliminates this problem entirely. If your drum currently has stuck foam, run a hot rinse cycle with no clothes and no detergent to flush it out, then start fresh with the correct dose.

## 7. Water Level Sensor (Pressure Switch) Fault

The water level sensor, also called the pressure switch, tells the machine's control board when the drum has emptied. A thin pressure hose connects it to the drum; if that hose develops a pinhole leak, gets kinked, or fills with gunk, the sensor never registers "empty" and the machine refuses to advance past the drain cycle. This fault is sneaky because the pump may actually be working fine but the control board does not know it. A technician will blow through the pressure hose to clear it, check the switch with a multimeter, and replace whichever component has failed. Most pressure switch replacements are completed in a single visit and come with a six-month warranty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I fix a washing machine that is not draining myself?

Yes for the simple causes — clogged filter, kinked hose, overdosing — these are safe DIY checks. For pump, switch or sensor faults, call a technician. Working inside a washer without training risks electrical shock and water damage. See our [FAQ page](/faq/) for more safety guidance.

### Will it damage my machine if I keep using it without fixing the drain?

Yes. Continued use forces the pump to run dry, overheats the motor, and can short the control board — turning a ₹2,000 fix into a ₹8,000 one. Stop using the machine the moment you notice drain failures.
